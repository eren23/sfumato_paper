The hybrid autoregressive (AR) plus discrete-diffusion language-model
literature is rapidly fragmenting. BD3-LMs~\citep{nie2025bd3} train
block-AR + within-block diffusion at $400$M+ scale. Transfusion
\citep{zhou2024transfusion} trains a single transformer with both AR
and diffusion heads at $7$B for multimodal data. DiffuLLaMA
\citep{gong2024diffullama} adapts a pre-trained AR backbone to
diffusion mode at $127$M--$7$B. ELF~\citep{chen2026elf} demonstrates
pure continuous flow language models. DiFFPO~\citep{wei2025diffpo}
suggests discrete diffusion outperforms AR \emph{in data-constrained
settings} via reinforcement-learning fine-tuning.

None of these works isolates the \emph{joint-training} hypothesis
against a \emph{parameter-matched, separately-trained} baseline at the
small scale ($\sim 60$--$300$M parameters) that academic and hobbyist
budgets actually access. Concretely: does a single shared-backbone
transformer trained with an interpolated AR + diffusion loss learn
something the same-parameter-count two-separate-trunk baseline does
not? At what data regime, on what axes, and at what cost?

This paper answers that question with a small-substrate
characterisation study, then extends it into an inference-recipe
contribution. We train composite, AR-only, diffusion-only, and
paired-separate-trunk variants from scratch on GSM8K-train
\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} at $60$M--$300$M parameters, then scale up
to a $305$M run (F10) on a $95\%$ FineWeb-Edu + $5\%$ GSM8K Q/A
mixed stream for $3$B tokens of training.

We measure six axes:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{AR-axis perplexity} on held-out GSM8K
        (composite vs.\ AR-only).
  \item \textbf{Diffusion-axis perplexity} with a
        \textbf{compute-matched control} where pure-diffusion gets
        $2\times$ training steps.
  \item \textbf{Data-efficiency crossover}: where in problem-count
        space does the trade reverse?
  \item \textbf{Mode-switching inference}: AR-then-diffusion-revise
        downstream accuracy on GSM8K-dev.
  \item \textbf{Composite-specific structural capabilities at $305$M}:
        parallel-decode speed, FIM infilling, revision NLL.
  \item \textbf{Per-token cross-mode routing (Phase K)}: a novel
        recipe in which diff drafts $k$ tokens in parallel and AR
        refills only the percentile-bottom-$K\%$ by commit-time
        confidence; with an $\alpha$-sensitivity sweep over the
        diffusion training fraction.
\end{enumerate}

Our central findings are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Composite \textbf{beats compute-matched pure diffusion} by
        $-0.69$ NLL at $200$M --- joint training is structural, not
        an artefact of more total gradient signal. The advantage
        replicates and grows with scale ($-0.21$ at $120$M to
        $-0.74$ at $300$M).
  \item A \textbf{data-efficiency crossover} whose sign change
        localises to the $800$--$1{,}200$ problem band: composite wins
        by $-0.40$ NLL at $500$ problems (composite lower for all $3$
        seeds; paired $t(2)$ diagnostic); loses by $+0.22$ NLL at the
        full $7{,}473$.
  \item At $305$M, composite delivers a $\mathbf{5.43\times}$
        parallel-decode speedup, a structurally unique FIM
        capability, and a $-0.74$ NLL/token revision via
        mode-switch (\textsection\ref{sec:phase-j}).
  \item \textbf{Phase K — per-token diff-draft + AR-refill}: at
        F10 with $N=200$ held-out problems, single-round \code{pct50}
        beats pure AR by $\mathbf{-0.58}$ NLL/token. A diff-heavy
        fine-tune (F11, $\alpha\!=\!0.30$ fixed) adds a further
        $\mathbf{-0.58}$ NLL/token. The $\alpha$-sweep
        ($\{0.20, 0.30, 0.40\}$) identifies $\alpha\!=\!0.30$ as
        the Pareto-optimum for K.2 NLL + AR-axis accuracy
        (\textsection\ref{sec:phase-k}).
  \item \textbf{Honest negatives.} Composite is small-uniformly
        worse on OOD perplexity ($+0.1$--$0.2$ NLL) and slightly
        worse-calibrated at $4$ of $5$ scales. SFT on GSM8K-train
        learns the answer \emph{format} but not the arithmetic
        (Phase L). A REINFORCE-trained mode router (frozen backbone,
        $526$K parameter MLP) collapses loop rate ($0.69 \to 0.00$)
        but does not lift accuracy (Phase I.3). Exclusive Self
        Attention (XSA) as a drop-in attention mod shows no
        benefit at $60$M (Phase N.1).
  \item As a secondary diagnostic finding, free-run sample quality
        at FineWeb-Edu scale ($305$M $/$ $3$B tokens) fails despite
        a teacher-forced \arNLL{}$=3.96$ that would otherwise read
        as clean. We trace this to three independent root causes
        --- decoder wiring, prompt-format OOD, Chinchilla undertrain
        --- and apply two decoder-side fixes that collapse free-run
        loop rates by $40$--$60$ pp without retraining
        (\textsection\ref{sec:sample-quality}).
\end{itemize}

We position the result as a \textbf{trade-off characterisation with
a deployable inference recipe}. Phase K's per-token cross-mode
routing turns the composite's structural diff-axis advantage into a
concrete win at inference: better per-token NLL on gold continuations
\emph{and} faster generation than pure AR, on a single composite
model. $\alpha\!=\!0.30$ is the recommended training recipe; K.2
\code{pct50} is the recommended decoder.
