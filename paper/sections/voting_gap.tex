\section{The Voting-Rule Gap and Verifier Aggregation}
\label{sec:voting_gap}

The previous sections rule out generic AR self-consistency as the
explanation for \LLaDA{}'s \cmaj{} advantage and characterize three
axes of trainable hybrid failure.  A different question --- and the
single most actionable observation in our follow-up phase --- is
whether \cmaj{} itself is leaving accuracy on the table.  It is.
Across every \cmaj{} configuration evaluated, the \emph{oracle}
ceiling (mark a problem solved if any of the $b{=}5$ branches is
correct) exceeds the \cmaj{} majority vote by $8\text{--}12$\,pp.
This section reports that gap, two negative results on closing it
with self-trained per-branch verifiers, the encoder-scaling trend
that survives those negatives, and a positive control with a
frontier-class judge that closes $86\%$ of the gap.  The mechanism
that ties the four findings together is that \LLaDA{}'s failure mode
on GSM8K is problem-comprehension, not arithmetic.

\subsection{Multi-Seed Variance Bar}
\label{subsec:multiseed}

Seeds $1$ and $2$ in Table~\ref{tab:voting_gap} were also run for the
same purpose as Phase-1 paper-hardening: to rule out that the
$82.5\%$ \cmajc{} headline (seed $0$, $N{=}200$, Track-1-v3 $+$
commit-v3) was a seed-dependent artifact.  Three-seed mean
$\mathbf{82.2\%}$, sample standard deviation $\mathbf{0.29}$\,pp ---
tighter than the Wilson interval at $N{=}100$.  The Phase-1
\cmajc{} headline is robust to seed selection; this closes the
multi-seed variance exposure originally listed in
\appref{app:open}.

\subsection{Per-Branch Verifiers: Seventeen Architectures, All Lose}
\label{subsec:verifier_negatives}

The $8\text{--}12$\,pp gap is enough to motivate a per-branch
verifier that reranks the five \cmaj{} branches at inference time.
We pre-registered a success threshold of $\geq 83\%$ mean accuracy
on $5$-fold cross-validation split by \code{problem\_id} (to
prevent leakage on the same problem appearing in train and eval
folds).  Across $17$ architectures spanning four orders of magnitude
in encoder size and four distinct architecture families ---
text-bag classifiers, chat-LM mean-pool encoders, embedding-specific
models, math-tuned chat models, process-feature MLPs, contrastive
branch-pair models, step-level PRMs, and a symbolic-arithmetic
checker --- \emph{every} architecture underperformed the \cmaj{}
baseline.  Table~\ref{tab:verifier_top} summarizes the top of the
leaderboard; the full ranking ($23$ entries) is released as
\code{phase2/RANKING.md} alongside the per-spike result JSONs.

\subsection{Three Robust Negative Findings}
\label{subsec:three_negatives}

Across the leaderboard three patterns are stable enough to call out
as findings on their own.

\paragraph{Math-tuning hurts as a verifier.}
Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Qwen2.5-Math-RM-72B both underperform their
non-math-tuned counterparts at the same parameter count ($-6.5$\,pp
vs $-4.0$\,pp for Qwen2.5-7B chat).  Math-specific fine-tuning
appears to compress features around math \emph{vocabulary} (``is
this math?''), losing the discrimination needed to rank
sibling-but-different math-CoT branches by correctness (``is this
math right?'').

\paragraph{Embedding-specific models lose worst.}
BGE-M3, Qwen3-Embedding-4B/8B, and gte-Qwen2-7B all underperform
chat-LMs of the same size as feature extractors.  Qwen3-Embedding-8B
($-8$\,pp) is dominated by the \emph{smaller} Qwen3-8B chat
($-5.5$\,pp).  Cosine/InfoNCE training compresses embeddings around
\emph{similarity}, which is the wrong invariant: branches differing
only in problem setup are similar by construction.

\paragraph{Doubling the substrate makes verifiers worse.}
Holding architecture fixed and increasing $N$ from $200$ to $500$
moves $\Delta_\text{pp}$ in the wrong direction --- $-5.5 \to -6.16$
for the process-MLP and $-8.98$ for the symbolic checker (vs
$-8$\,pp at $N{=}200$).  This is the signature of an
overfit-to-substrate-noise regime, and is consistent with the
$8\text{--}12$\,pp gap being structural rather than data-bound.

\subsection{A Frontier Judge with CoT Closes 86\% of the Gap}
\label{subsec:frontier_judge}

The negative results above are bounded by an explicit positive
control.  We call Claude Sonnet 4.5~\citep{anthropic2025sonnet}
through the OpenRouter API~\citep{openrouter2024} once per branch,
under two prompts: a YES/NO verdict prompt and a
chain-of-thought-then-verdict prompt that forces the judge to
reconstruct the problem setup before judging the candidate solution.
Substrate is the GSM8K-test $N{=}500$ extension ($200$ frozen indices
$+$ $300$ fresh, $b{=}5$, $t{=}0.7$, Track-1-v3 LoRA).

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
Hand inspection of the $15$ \cmaj{}-failed/oracle-recoverable
problems on the $N{=}500$ substrate reveals that wrong branches
typically contain $100\%$ correct arithmetic but apply it to
incorrect problem setups.  Figure~\ref{fig:cmaj_anatomy}
illustrates the failure mode on a representative problem
(GSM8K-test problem $1071$).  All five branches do correct
arithmetic; three branches misread ``\$6 for each \emph{additional}
guest'' as ``\$6 for each guest'' and arrive at a self-consistent
\$$371$; two branches read the constraint correctly and arrive
at \$$251$.  Majority vote picks the wrong setup; the oracle
ceiling and a frontier judge that re-derives the constraint both
recover the correct answer.

This single observation predicts the entire leaderboard:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Symbolic verifiers lose} ($-8.98$\,pp): every
          branch's arithmetic statement-checks pass equally,
          because the arithmetic is correct everywhere.
    \item \textbf{Peer-class neural verifiers lose} (all $17$):
          they cannot distinguish wrong-setup-with-right-arithmetic
          from right-setup-with-right-arithmetic without external
          problem-decomposition reasoning the encoder does not
          have.
    \item \textbf{Frontier judges win}: at this scale, the judge
          is strictly stronger at problem decomposition than the
          \LLaDA{}-$8$B generator, which is what the verification
          task actually requires.
    \item \textbf{CoT pushes the judge from inconclusive to
          WIN-MINOR}: forcing the judge to reconstruct the problem
          setup from scratch (rather than pattern-matching the
          candidate solution) recovers $5$ additional cmaj-failed
          problems ($8 \to 13$ of $15$).
\end{enumerate}

This is consistent with the verifier literature: \citet{cobbe2021verifier}
trained a fine-tuned GPT-3 verifier for GSM8K, and
\citet{lightman2023letsverify} used step-level human supervision
(PRM800K) to outperform outcome-reward classifiers at scale.  Both
results are consistent with our reading: a verifier that beats
majority vote requires either substantially stronger reasoning than
the generator or per-step human-quality supervision.

\subsection{Substrate Reconciliation}
\label{subsec:substrate_reconciliation}

The frontier-judge result in \S\ref{subsec:frontier_judge} runs on
GSM8K-test $N{=}500$, an extension of the $N{=}200$ frozen index set
used everywhere else in the paper ($300$ fresh indices added on top
of the original $200$).  The base \cmaj{} baseline on this larger
substrate moves from $79.0\%$ on $N{=}200$ to $79.1\%$ on $N{=}500$,
a difference well within sampling noise and well within the binomial
CI at either size.  All other headline numbers in the paper remain
on the original $N{=}200$ substrate; the larger substrate is used
only where the per-call OpenRouter cost made the larger sample
worth running.

\subsection{What Closes Here, What is Phase-3}
\label{subsec:voting_gap_takeaway}

Two open exposures from \appref{app:open} close on the strength of
the work in this section: the multi-seed variance bar
(\S\ref{subsec:multiseed}) and the question of whether a per-branch
reranker can substitute for the param-time commit
(\S\ref{subsec:verifier_negatives}, \S\ref{subsec:frontier_judge};
the answer is ``not at peer-class scale'').  An ABL\_B sanity probe
from the same exposure list~\citep{abl_b_result} confirms the v3
commit-LoRA modifies inference at temperature $0$ on $5/5$ spot-check
problems (\code{\#\#\#\#\,N} $\to$ \code{Answer:\,N} format shift),
ruling out a silent-no-op reading of the v3 commit; we fold this
into the appendix update rather than into the body table.

The remaining open question --- whether a self-trained verifier can
close the $8\text{--}12$\,pp gap --- is genuinely Phase-3.  The
monotone encoder-scaling trend leaves the door open for a $32$B$+$
chat-LM encoder; the Workstream-C STATUS-schema traces released
alongside this paper are the substrate for a process-reward
verifier that consumes per-step trajectory features (per-position
entropy, commit-LoRA logit shifts, AR/DDLM mechanism source) rather
than per-branch surface features; and PRM800K-style human
step-level supervision~\citep{lightman2023letsverify} is the
proven-elsewhere recipe at scale.  We leave all three to follow-up
work and document the gap, the $17$ negatives, and the frontier
judge here so that the direction is flagged as productive rather
than closed.
