\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Reconciling the Conflicting Reports}
\label{subsec:reconciling}

The single most useful sentence in this paper, and the one we
would ask a reader to remember, is that
\textbf{\LLaDA{}'s GSM8K failure mode is problem comprehension,
not arithmetic.}  Hand inspection of the $15$
\cmaj{}-failed/oracle-recoverable problems
(\S\ref{sec:voting_gap}) shows that wrong branches typically
contain locally correct arithmetic applied to incorrect problem
\emph{setups} --- the model misreads what is being asked, not
what to compute.  Reading the rest of this discussion through
that lens collapses the apparently disjoint findings of the body
into a single picture.

\paragraph{A direct test of the comprehension hypothesis.}  As a
follow-up mechanism probe (not a deployment recipe), we asked
Claude Sonnet $4.5$ to convert each GSM$8$K-dev question into a
structured restatement --- a list of givens, an explicit unknown,
and a list of named intermediate quantities to compute, with
explicit instructions \emph{not} to compute any numerical answer.
We then ran the v3 hybrid stack
($\code{LLaDA-8B-Instruct} + $ prefix-robust v3 LoRA $+$ commit-v3
LoRA, single branch, $k{=}64$, $T{=}0$, commit-$n${=}$3$) with the
Sonnet restatement substituted for the raw question.  On
$N{=}100$ frozen-index problems, the scaffolded run reaches
$84.0\%$, against $75.0\%$ for the same v3 stack on the raw
question and the same seed (a $+9.0$\,pp delta on an
apples-to-apples comparison).  This single-branch scaffolded
number beats the multi-branch \cmajc{}-v3 result
($82.5\%$ at $b{=}5$) and lands within $2.5$\,pp of the
parameter-matched plain-AR \Qwen{}-$2.5$-$7$B baseline
($86.5\%$).  We surface this as a \emph{mechanism probe}, not
a deployable recipe: Sonnet $4.5$ solo scores
$\approx 95\text{--}97\%$ on GSM$8$K, so the hybrid pipeline
strictly underperforms just calling Sonnet on its own.  The
publishable bit is the $+9$\,pp jump under same-stack matched
comparison, which quantifies the fraction of the
\LLaDA{}-on-GSM$8$K gap that is upstream of arithmetic --- not
that the hybrid is a useful product.  This complements the
verifier-gap diagnosis in \S\ref{sec:voting_gap}: where the
frontier-judge result shows the gap is closable post-hoc, the
scaffolded-prompt result shows part of it is closable
\emph{pre-hoc} by repairing the comprehension step before the
diffusion sampler ever runs.

The three-axis decomposition lets us reconcile the conflicting
reports in the hybrid AR/DDLM literature.  Papers that report ``AR
planning helps DDLM'' likely measured the planner-content-trust
axis with a well-matched planner-and-adapter capacity --- i.e.\ a
configuration where the upstream planner produces content the DDLM
trusts at the prevailing capacity setting.  Papers that report
``AR planning hurts DDLM'' likely measured the format-brittleness
axis without controlling for prefix shape: prepending a plan-shaped
scaffold of \emph{any} content ($66\text{--}68\%$ at base, versus
$74\%$ for no prefix) is enough to land in the
``hurts'' regime.  Papers that report ``diffusion sampling
diversity is preserved'' likely measured the right thing under a
recipe that lands at v2-style capacity; papers that report
``diversity collapses'' likely had a different recipe that pushed
past the capacity sweet spot we identify in
Table~\ref{tab:axis1_v2_v3}.

Without the three-axis frame, these are five conflicting claims.
Within the frame, they are five projections of a multi-axis
failure surface, each consistent with the others when the omitted
axes are held fixed.

The same lens predicts the structure of the entire verifier
leaderboard in \S\ref{sec:voting_gap}.  Why do peer-class
chat-LMs lose ($-4$ to $-14$\,pp)?  Because the verification task
is implicitly ``re-solve the problem from scratch and compare''
and a peer-class classifier without that capability has nothing
to add over majority vote.  Why do math-tuned encoders lose
\emph{worse} than chat-tuned ones at the same scale (math vocab
is irrelevant to the failure mode)?  Because arithmetic vocabulary
is the wrong inductive bias when the failure is upstream of
arithmetic.  Why do embedding models lose worst (cosine
similarity collapses problem distinctions a classifier needs)?
Same.  Why does a frontier judge with chain-of-thought win
($+6.16$\,pp, $86\%$ gap-closure)?  Because at this scale it is
strictly stronger at the problem-decomposition step than
\LLaDA{}-$8$B is.  And why does the chain-of-thought variant beat
the YES/NO prompt by $5$ recovered problems
($8 \to 13$ of $15$)?  Because forcing the judge to re-derive the
setup is forcing it to re-do the step the generator failed.  This
is consistent with a verifier literature in which Cobbe et
al.~\citep{cobbe2021verifier} used a fine-tuned GPT-$3$ verifier
and Lightman et al.~\citep{lightman2023letsverify} used
step-level human supervision: when the verification task is
implicitly to re-solve and compare, a peer-class classifier with
surface features cannot win, and a stronger reasoner can.

\subsection{Parallel Negatives Across Aggregation Sub-Problems}
\label{subsec:parallel_negatives}

The voting-rule gap (\S\ref{sec:voting_gap}) and the offline-replay
mode router (\S\ref{sec:mode_router}) probe two different
sub-problems of test-time aggregation in diffusion LMs --- per-branch
verification and per-problem mode routing --- and both fall to the
same pathology: at our substrate scale, supervised classification
with surface features cannot close the oracle gap.  Three
additional spikes probe further parallel sub-problems and land in
the same regime, which is the single piece of evidence we want to
surface here.

\paragraph{Schedule-conditional Temporal-SC voting.}
A schedule-weighted vote across denoising sub-blocks
($1.5\times$ on active sub-blocks, $1.0\times$ on inactive)
regressed $-4$\,pp on GSM8K dev$_{200}$ at $N{=}100$
(\cmajc{}-vote $0.82$, Temporal-SC $0.78$).  The
canonical answer span (\code{\#\#\#\#}, \code{Answer:}) only
materializes at sub-block~$3$ of $4$ in $94\%$ of branches, so the
$1.5\times$ weight on sub-blocks~$1$--$2$ amplifies a near-empty
channel.  Zhang et al.~\citep{zhang2025timefeature} report a
positive temporal-SC result on diffusion LMs with an
exponential-decay weighting; our schedule-conditional weighting
does not extend their result.

\paragraph{Schedule-RLHF on commit-LoRA.}
A mini-pilot ($50$-step GRPO, no KL anchor) was paired
$-1$\,problem on MATH-$500$ at $N{=}20$; a mid-pilot ($200$-step
GRPO with a Schulman-$k_3$ KL anchor at $\beta{=}0.05$) was paired
$+1$\,problem on MATH-$500$ at $N{=}50$, with $49/50$ predictions
bit-identical and one wrong-to-correct flip.  Both signals are
within binomial CI ($\pm 14$\,pp at $N{=}50$); the meaningful
evidence is the directional reversal once the KL anchor is wired,
which says schedule-conditional adapter retraining is at minimum
non-destructive in this regime.  We do not claim a WIN on the
mid-pilot signal alone; a locked pre-registered full pilot at
$3100$ steps over $1550$ prompts is the gating experiment.

\paragraph{Cross-base-model transfer.}
A drop-in swap from \LLaDA{}-$8$B-Instruct~\citep{nie2025llada} to
LLaDA-$1.5$ (the VRPO post-trained successor) with the frozen v$3$
LoRAs landed MATH-$500$-numeric $N{=}50$ \cmajc{}-$k_3$ $= 0.56$,
$-2$\,pp below the LLaDA-$8$B-Instruct reference of $0.58$.
Adapter compatibility passed cleanly --- the swap is mechanically
a no-op --- but capability transfer failed.  The peer-class
accuracy gap discussed in \S\ref{subsec:ar_baseline} is not
explained by base-model VRPO post-training.

\medskip

The unifying read across \S\ref{sec:voting_gap},
\S\ref{sec:mode_router}, and the three spikes above is that
test-time aggregation in diffusion LMs is not a
single-classifier or single-toggle problem at the data scales we
can afford.  The three follow-ups in \S\ref{sec:discussion} Future
Work --- a substantially larger encoder, a process-reward verifier
on per-step trajectory features, or step-level human supervision
---  apply to every parallel sub-problem we have probed.

\subsection{Honest Peer Baseline: Plain AR at Matched Scale}
\label{subsec:ar_baseline}

The hybrid pipeline this paper characterises has $\sim 9$B active
parameters at inference (\LLaDA{}-$8$B $+$ Track-$1$-v3 LoRA $+$
commit-v3 LoRA $+$ a Qwen-\{$0.5$B, $1.5$B\} planner) and reaches
\cmajc{} $\approx 82.5\%$ on GSM8K-test.  A plain Qwen-$2.5$-$7$B
AR baseline on the same evaluation reaches approximately $86.5\%$,
beating the hybrid at strictly fewer active parameters.  Hybrid
accuracy is also roughly invariant ($82\text{--}83\%$) across
Qwen-$0.5$B through Qwen-$7$B planners, which is consistent with
the diffusion substrate (\LLaDA{}-$8$B) rather than the AR planner
being the bottleneck on this benchmark.

We surface this here rather than burying it.  The three trainable
axes (\S\ref{sec:axis1}--\S\ref{sec:axis3}), the consensus
distillation result (\S\ref{sec:distill}), the voting-rule gap
(\S\ref{sec:voting_gap}), and the K2 schedule toggle
(\S\ref{sec:commit_lora_k2}) are real empirical findings about the
AR/DDLM substrate as currently constituted.  They are not evidence
that this hybrid pipeline beats a parameter-matched monolithic AR
baseline on GSM8K; at the AR scales we used as planners, it does
not.  The natural read is that the substrate-level findings should
generalise to AR/DDLM stacks where the diffusion model is closer
to AR peer-class scale, and that the practical accuracy claim of
\emph{this} hybrid against a parameter-matched AR is benchmark- and
scale-bounded.  We treat the empirical AR-baseline number as
disclosure rather than a target, and leave a head-to-head
controlled comparison at peer-class diffusion scale to follow-up
work.

\subsection{Implication: Distillation of Inference-Time Mechanisms}

The Track 2 design-iteration finding has a broader implication:
weight-space distillation of inference-time mechanisms is
design-sensitive in a way that produces clean negative results
that masquerade as architectural impossibilities.

Distilling a technique that aggregates over multiple stochastic
samples (\cmaj{}) into a single deterministic forward pass
requires the surgery to match the temporal structure of the
original mechanism.  For semi-AR diffusion, ``temporal structure''
means \emph{which sub-block fires the modification}.  Getting this
wrong (last-block-only when the answer is upstream of the
modification) produces \ctwoc{} $= 70.5\%$ across a $3.25\times$
capacity bump --- a clean negative whose surface reading is ``the
adapter cannot do this''.  Getting it right (commit on sub-blocks
$2$--$4$ of $4$) recovers \ctwoc{} $= 79.0\%$, within sampling
error of the target, on the same data.  The architecture did not
change; the temporal placement did.

This is a general lesson for parameter-efficient distillation of
sampling-based mechanisms: the placement of the LoRA in the
sampler's schedule is a first-class design variable, and a
plateau across capacity is not by itself evidence that the
distillation is impossible.

\subsection{Future Work}

\paragraph{Cross-task transfer.}  Do these axes generalize beyond
GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}?  A targeted ablation on MATH-Easy
and ARC-Challenge would test whether the three-axis decomposition
holds on other reasoning benchmarks.  The diversity-expansion
finding in particular is suspicious-looking on a single benchmark
and deserves a multi-task replication.

\paragraph{Phase-3 verifier paths.}  The voting-rule-gap section
rules out small per-branch classifiers as a \emph{compute-time}
alternative to v3's \emph{param-time} commit at the scales we
tested ($17$ peer-class architectures, all LOSS).  Three paths
remain open for closing the residual $8\text{--}12$\,pp gap:
(i)~a $32$B$+$ chat-LM encoder, motivated by the monotone
encoder-scaling trend ($\sim 5$\,pp gap-closure per $10\times$
scale-up at $0.5$B--$7$B); (ii)~a process-reward verifier consuming
per-step trajectory features (per-position entropy, commit-LoRA
logit shifts, AR/DDLM mechanism source) over the Workstream-C
STATUS-schema traces released alongside this paper; and
(iii)~step-level PRM800K-style human
supervision~\citep{lightman2023letsverify}, the proven-elsewhere
recipe at the cost of step-level annotation.  The frontier-judge
positive control (\S\ref{subsec:frontier_judge}) bounds the
reachable accuracy at $\sim 85\%$ on this substrate; the question
is whether (i)--(iii) reach the bound at lower API cost.

\paragraph{Diversity-mechanism test.}  Does base \LLaDA{} at
$t{=}0.9$ reproduce the post-Track-1 $5/5$-same rate of $47.5\%$?
If yes, Track 1 acts as an implicit temperature regularizer and
the diversity-expansion finding reduces to that.  If no, the
training-time surface randomization has produced real
content-level diversity that temperature does not match.  One
short eval; deferred to revision.

\paragraph{Content-trust adapter.}  The planner-content-trust
phenomenon (\S\ref{sec:axis2}) is characterized but not fixed,
and would benefit from a third capacity rung before being framed
as a fully-resolved axis.  A
content-trust LoRA --- one that varies plan content quality at
fixed surface shape, complementing our prefix-robust dataset
which varies surface shape at fixed content --- is a natural
follow-up, especially with \Qwen{1.5B} and \Qwen{7B} planners
sweeping the upstream-content axis explicitly.

\paragraph{Adaptive mode router (Paper 2).}  The
structural-separation finding motivates a learned router that
switches between mode-specific LoRAs based on input.  The
capacity-tradeoff curve (v2 vs.\ v3) gives the router a real
decision space: given an input prompt, which adapter should fire?
We sketched the design in \code{sfumato/PLAN.md} as the E1 track.
\S\ref{sec:mode_router} reports an offline-replay, per-problem
form of E1 evaluated within Phase 2: at $20$ problems and a
$12$-condition action space with surface text features it
underperforms the best fixed condition by $10$\,pp, with the same
small-$N$/large-action/surface-feature pathology as the verifier
sweep.  Two \$0 pre-scale audits
(\S\ref{subsec:mode_router_pre_scale}) further narrow what a
trajectory-feature router could possibly look like: on the
$N{=}200$ \cmaj{} $b{=}5$ substrate, $91.7\%$ of disagreement
problems first diverge at sub-block~$0$ (token edit-distance,
$88.9\%$ semantic-cosine cross-check, $95.4\%$ agreement), so
there is essentially no mid-trajectory decision point for a
\emph{per-boundary} router to operate on; on the seven existing
STATUS-schema traces, the trajectory-feature signal that exists
($\rho{=}{-}0.79$ for \code{min\_entropy}, perm-$p{=}0.097$ at
$n{=}7$, a hint not an evaluation) is concentrated at
sub-block~$0$.  The two viable forms the audits leave open are
(i)~a problem-level pre-generation router using
\emph{trajectory} rather than surface-text features (untested),
and (ii)~a ``pilot-and-route'' architecture that probes
sub-block~$0$, extracts features, and routes once.  Per-boundary
routing across all four sub-blocks is empirically dead at this
substrate.  We leave the pilot-and-route variant to follow-up
work.

\subsection{Limitations}

We close with what this paper does not do.  All evaluations are
single-seed at $N{=}200$, so our headline numbers carry binomial
sampling noise of $\pm 3$--$5$\,pp; multi-seed variance is not
characterized in this draft.  We did not produce a mechanistic
LCH-centroid figure for the
prefix-robust LoRA, which would harden the structural-separation
thesis from behavioural to mechanistic; the JVP feasibility spike
failed because PEFT wraps LoRA in
\code{lora\_magnitude\_vector} ModuleDict and we deferred to a
follow-up.  All experiments use a single base diffusion model
(\LLaDA{}-8B-Instruct~\citep{nie2025llada}) and a single
benchmark (GSM8K-test~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}); the
generalization claims in the abstract are constrained
accordingly.  These exposures and a few smaller ones are listed in
\appref{app:open}.
